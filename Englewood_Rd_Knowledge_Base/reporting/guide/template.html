How the house gets its heat

The Polaris tanks do the job often assigned to a boiler: they heat water for space heating. But they are potable-water heaters, and the household hot-water system shares that same water. The floor circuits receive blended water; the air-handler coils receive water from the heating supply branches.

01 / MAKE & STORE HEAT

The tanks work in series

The common heating return enters the left Polaris. Water passes from its Heating System Outlet into the right tank’s Heating System Inlet. The right tank then supplies the heating branches.

02 / MOVE & MIX WATER

Several pumps, different jobs

The Grundfos moves water around the main loop. Mix 1 and Mix 2 circulate radiant water, while Var 1 and Var 2 add heat by injection. Basement radiant has its own pump and mechanical mixer.

03 / DELIVER HEAT

Floors first, warm air as support

Zone valves select the first-floor and upstairs radiant branches. Two Lennox AM30 units use water coils and blowers for supplemental forced-air heating. Domestic water leaves through a separate tempering valve.

The three upstairs pumps are not interchangeable roles. The Grundfos supplies the main loop; Var 2 injects heat into the radiant circuit; Mix 2 circulates that circuit. A fourth pump, inside AHU-2, serves the air-handler coil. The current Mix 2 demand problem and the suspected AHU-2 pump problem are separate investigations.
What “confirmed,” “inferred,” and “unknown” mean here

Confirmed means a photographed identity, recorded measurement, or homeowner observation/physical trace. It does not mean every connection has been independently tested. Inferred means the arrangement fits the available observations but its cable or operating sequence has not been verified end to end. Unknown stays visible rather than being filled in from a typical installation.

The drawings are explanatory schematics of the reported installation, not scaled construction drawings. Routine isolation valves, drains, unions, and hidden fittings are omitted unless needed to explain a circuit. Crossing lines connect only where a junction dot appears. Detailed duct branches and air-damper assignments are outside the agreed scope.

Why use injection mixing?

The floor loop recirculates water through its own Mix pump. The Var pump introduces a controlled amount of hotter main-loop water. Water also leaves the mixed circuit through its return connection. This lets the floor circulation and the added heat be controlled separately.

Both reported supply probes sit just upstream of their Mix pumps. Their placement is drawn as found, without relocating them to a textbook arrangement.

Two Caleffi valves, two different cold sources

Basement TMV-1: cooler water comes from the basement radiant return. It blends with the hot heating supply before the basement Taco.

Domestic TMV-2: cold water comes from the street-water branch. It tempers the right tank’s domestic hot outlet before distribution and passive recirculation.

What the relay tracing established—and what it did not

The checked black relay circuits share this pattern: ZCM THERMOSTAT terminal 2 → relay lug 2 by a coiled red lead; ZCM THERMOSTAT terminal 3 → relay lug 4 by a white lead. The checked SB and 4 positions are unused. This is the upper thermostat strip, not the lower valve-feedback strip.

A white transformer conductor bridges the brown Honeywell relay and the two upper black relays, apparently as their common. The brown Honeywell R8222D1014 switches line voltage to the basement radiant Taco. Its incoming control cable endpoint remains unverified. A shared wire or matching wire color does not establish all relay functions.

The upper pair’s photographed models are MARS 90293 and White-Rodgers 90-293Q, with 24 VAC coils. The lower relay identities and complete installed coil/contact assignments remain open. The report deliberately avoids drawing a completed thermostat-to-relay ladder from these partial traces.

A working first-floor radiant call

  1. The room asks for heat. Its ZCM-1 yellow indicator illuminates; the exact external relay/staging route is not fully traced.
  2. The zone valve opens. In the Living Room comparison, the isolated auxiliary end switch changed from OL to continuity.
  3. ZCM-1’s red indicator was observed on; voltage across ES fell from 27.2 VAC to approximately 1 VAC. MC-1 indicated Mix 1 demand.
  4. MC-1’s Mix 1 output was observed active. The Grundfos is switched by Boiler Enable; Var 1 regulates injected heat according to controller logic and sensor conditions.
  5. Mix 1 circulates through the open zone, and the manifold returns join the shared first-floor return. The circuit exchanges water with the returning main loop.

Observed measurements and wiring are combined here into a functional explanation. Exact controller delays and injection behavior were not logged.

Where the upstairs sequence is breaking

  1. Either upstairs thermostat calls; the matching ZCM-2 yellow indicator lights.
  2. The Honeywell actuator visibly moves toward open. That demonstrates motion, not necessarily full travel or successful auxiliary switch actuation.
  3. At both upstairs valves, the isolated end-switch pair stayed OL during the motor-driven call. The working Living Room comparison did show continuity.
  4. ZCM-2 red remained off, ES remained around 27 VAC, and MC-1 showed no Mix 2 demand under the observed conditions.
  5. Mix 2’s output did not activate. The next diagnostic priority is valve travel / end-switch actuation, followed by verifying ZCM-2 response to valid feedback.

A defective ZCM-2 has not been established. Connected R1/R2 falling to 0 VAC did not prove feedback closure.

One independent basement radiant zone

  1. Basement heating demand reaches the dedicated Honeywell switching relay; the complete thermostat-control route remains untraced.
  2. The relay supplies 120 V to the basement Taco. There is no basement radiant ZCM in the reported installation.
  3. TMV-1 blends hot water from the right Polaris heating outlet with basement floor return.
  4. The Taco sends blended water straight to the basement supply manifold. Return water splits toward the mixer and the common heating return.
  5. This basement-only radiant call does not request MC-1 or start the Grundfos. The Polaris tanks provide heat through their own water-temperature controls.

Supplemental heat through an AM30 coil

  1. The owner describes stage 2 as forced-air support while stage 1 radiant remains on. Stage overlap has not been verified from thermostat settings or a complete trace.
  2. The appropriate air-zone panel is expected to request its AM30. The basement MABS-to-AHU-1 and attic EMM-3-to-AHU-2 cable identities remain inferred.
  3. The air handler’s internal water pump serves its heating coil; the blower moves air across it. Detailed duct zoning is outside this survey.
  4. AHU-1 has its own branch from the right tank. AHU-2 is on the upstairs main loop and depends on adequate hot-water delivery there.
  5. If upstairs radiant feedback never reaches MC-1, simultaneous stage 1 demand alone does not establish that the Grundfos is running. Any independent main-loop enable for an air-only call is still unresolved.

Domestic hot water without a radiant call

  1. Street cold water feeds the left Polaris Cold Inlet and the cold side of the domestic mixer.
  2. The right Polaris domestic hot outlet supplies TMV-2, which blends it with cold water for household use.
  3. The mixed outlet feeds domestic distribution. Owner-reported temperatures are approximately 170 °F in the right tank and 105–110 °F at the mixed outlet.
  4. The passive gravity return connects to the right tank’s lower Cold Inlet. No domestic recirculation pump was reported.
  5. The hot-water heaters respond to their own temperature controls. A household hot-water draw is not documented as an MC-1 heating demand.

Known issues & unfinished work

What needs attention—and what is only a hypothesis

This section carries forward the useful diagnostic findings from the earlier report. Equipment identity, observed symptoms, and proposed replacement parts remain separate.

ACTIVE · VALVE FEEDBACK PRIORITY

Upstairs radiant does not establish Mix 2 demand

Observed: both upstairs valves moved on a call, but their isolated end switches remained OL. A working Living Room valve changed from OL to continuity. ZCM-2 red stayed off; its ES pair remained approximately 27 VAC.

Interpretation: failure to provide valve-side feedback explains the missing-demand symptoms. Incomplete travel or switch actuation is the immediate focus. The results do not independently certify ZCM-2 as healthy or defective.

Next discriminator: resolve valve travel/end-switch operation, then confirm ZCM-2 ES transition and MC-1 demand/output with valid feedback.

ACTIVE · FAILURE MODE UNCONFIRMED

Attic AM30 internal circulator is suspected

Identity: Taco 006-B4-11 inside Lennox AM30Q2-40-2, serial 5801K48409. This pump is separate from the Taco 008-BC6 Mix 2 circulator.

Missing evidence: precise symptoms, electrical operation, actual flow, and the control sequence. A cartridge order should not turn a reported suspicion into a confirmed failure.

Parts problem: 006-040RP is the exact-model catalog cartridge, but an available approved successor has not been verified. The common 005-020RP listing explicitly excludes this pump.

A general diagnostic lesson about A303XX0003: voltage across connected lower TELESTAT R1/R2 is not a reliable standalone test of end-switch closure or board health. Both upstairs inputs eventually read 0 VAC even though the isolated valve end switches remained open. Approximately zero voltage across an isolated dry switch also says nothing about continuity; the working comparison was established with resistance/continuity measurement.

Pressure, temperature, shared-water requirements, and equipment history

The system was installed with the house in 1999. The current Grundfos production code indicates week 40 of 2012; that is not proof of its installation date. Both Polaris nameplates are recorded individually. Their plate marking is 160 °F outlet operation, while the owner reports operating the right tank around 170 °F.

The photographed gauge between Mix 1 and its three valves reads approximately 65 psi and 90 °F. It is a single uncalibrated snapshot, not a pressure survey. The 1999 Lennox CompleteHeat letter calls for an external pressure-reducing valve and thermal expansion tank and discusses pressures above 55 psi. That historical letter does not prove a reducing valve is installed today. The owner traced the cold feed and did not identify one; location/presence remains open.

Two expansion tanks are present. Their models, precharge, sizing, and condition are not documented. The AM30 plate requires potable-water-compatible components and prohibits adding boiler-treatment chemicals. The 450 psi marking on the C26 cooling-coil plate concerns refrigerant, not the shared water system.

Remaining major gaps in the system map
  • Full thermostat-stage routing through black relays, ZCMs, air-zone panels, and AM30 inputs.
  • Whether upstairs stage 2 retains stage 1, and whether any independent air-handler call can enable the Grundfos.
  • ZCM feedback logic and response to a verified closed valve switch; exact LED meanings and separate OUTPUT-strip function.
  • Installed DuoMix settings, delays, protections, and the selected LCD item that displayed OPEN. A matched DuoMix manual has not been established.
  • End-to-end identities for likely SUP 1, BRet, outdoor sensor, and MABS TL supply-air sensor cables.
  • Pressure-reducing valve presence, expansion tank details, and hidden check/isolation fittings.
  • Var 2’s glare-obscured model reading, remaining valve nameplates, and verified replacement availability.

None of these gaps prevents a useful high-level map. They do prevent treating every diagram line as a complete service schematic.

Terminology for a first-time visitor

Circulator

A pump moving water around a circuit. This system uses different pumps for main-loop delivery, radiant distribution, injection mixing, and air-handler coils.

Supply / return

Supply goes toward a load; return comes back. “Return” is relative: the returning main loop still provides heat to first-floor Var 1.

Manifold

A common header distributing to, or collecting from, multiple floor loops. A zone valve can control an entire manifold group.

End switch / ES

A valve’s auxiliary switch indicates completion of its powered opening stroke. The ZCM’s shared ES pair is a separate device-level demand contact. Neither is the air-zone panel’s damper terminal group.

Injection mixing

Adding hotter main-loop water to a recirculating floor circuit. Here “Var” names injection pumps and “Mix” names distribution pumps.

Brown thermostat cable

Brown-jacketed multiconductor thermostat cable. Several conductors in one jacket may serve different purposes, including a sensor circuit.
